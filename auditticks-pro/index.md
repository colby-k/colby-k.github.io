<section id="product" class="sp-statement">
  <p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">The product</p>
  <h2>Auditors should spend time evaluating evidence—not rebuilding the same documentation mechanics.</h2>
  <p>Tickmarks, legends, references, notes, callouts, formatting, and workpaper structures are small tasks individually. Repeated across samples and engagements, they create friction and inconsistency. AuditTicks Pro brings those actions into one purpose-built Excel ribbon.</p>
</section>

<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">What it solves</p><h2>Reduce the friction around audit documentation.</h2></div>
    <p>The product supports the mechanics of the workpaper. It does not determine the procedure, evaluate the evidence, or replace auditor judgment.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>01 · Repeated setup</span><h3>Stop rebuilding common audit elements.</h3><p>Apply standard tickmarks, legends, annotations, references, and workpaper structures from a dedicated ribbon.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>02 · Inconsistent conventions</span><h3>Use a common documentation language.</h3><p>Make symbols, explanations, tie-outs, notes, and formatting easier for preparers and reviewers to interpret.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>03 · Review friction</span><h3>Keep attention on evidence and conclusions.</h3><p>Reduce the time spent correcting presentation so review can focus on whether the audit work is responsive and supportable.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section sp-section--paper">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Feature groups</p><h2>Designed around recurring audit actions.</h2></div>
    <p>AuditTicks Pro organizes frequently used tools into a visible Excel ribbon so auditors can document the work without leaving the worksheet.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-feature-grid">
    <article><span>Document</span><h3>In-cell tickmarks</h3><p>Insert standardized symbols directly into selected cells, with configurable insertion and append behavior.</p></article>
    <article><span>Annotate</span><h3>Floating marks</h3><p>Place movable tickmarks, Roman numerals, circled characters, symbols, and tie-out references over the worksheet.</p></article>
    <article><span>Explain</span><h3>Notes and callouts</h3><p>Add arrows, braces, rectangles, callouts, font emphasis, and fill colors to make the work easier to follow.</p></article>
    <article><span>Standardize</span><h3>Legends and templates</h3><p>Generate legends and start with structured detailed, document, or schedule workpaper formats.</p></article>
    <article><span>Reference</span><h3>Tie-outs and cross-references</h3><p>Apply common audit references such as RC, SF, CR, TB, FS, and PY consistently.</p></article>
    <article><span>Navigate</span><h3>Workpaper utilities</h3><p>Use practical tools designed to reduce repetitive worksheet actions and support a cleaner audit file.</p></article>
    <article><span>Adopt</span><h3>Familiar Excel workflow</h3><p>Keep the work inside the environment many audit teams already use for testing and documentation.</p></article>
    <article><span>Improve</span><h3>Active product roadmap</h3><p>Continue refining the product through real use, feedback, maintenance, and controlled version releases.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section sp-section--dark">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker">Workpaper workflow</p><h2>From setup to review-ready documentation.</h2></div>
    <p>The add-in supports a repeatable workflow while leaving the auditor responsible for the substance of the audit procedure.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-timeline">
    <article><span>01 · Start</span><h3>Select the workpaper structure.</h3><p>Begin with a detailed testing, document-review, or schedule format rather than rebuilding the basic layout.</p></article>
    <article><span>02 · Test</span><h3>Perform the audit procedure.</h3><p>Document attributes, sample results, exceptions, evidence references, and the auditor’s evaluation.</p></article>
    <article><span>03 · Explain</span><h3>Apply consistent marks and context.</h3><p>Use tickmarks, notes, callouts, tie-outs, and legends to make the work understandable without excessive narrative.</p></article>
    <article><span>04 · Review</span><h3>Navigate a clearer audit record.</h3><p>Provide reviewers with a more consistent presentation so they can concentrate on risk, evidence, and conclusions.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Product principles</p><h2>Practical before clever.</h2></div>
    <p>The product succeeds only if it fits naturally into audit execution and makes the work easier to prepare and review.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card"><span>Excel-native</span><h3>Extend the tool auditors already use.</h3><p>Reduce context switching and adoption friction by placing the workflow directly inside Excel.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>Judgment-preserving</span><h3>Support documentation—not the conclusion.</h3><p>Auditors still define the procedure, evaluate the evidence, identify exceptions, and own the result.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>Review-oriented</span><h3>Design for preparers and reviewers.</h3><p>Use consistent conventions to make workpapers easier to navigate without forcing every audit into the same template.</p></article>
