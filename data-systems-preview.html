The challenge

A polished dashboard can still rest on unreliable data.

Analysis becomes difficult to defend when the population is incomplete, fields are inconsistent, transformation logic is hidden, or relationships are misunderstood. Data reliability is part of the audit work—not merely a technical prerequisite.

Where reliability breaks

Small data problems can become confident analytical conclusions.

01Incomplete

Missing records or fields distort the population being reviewed.

02Inconsistent

Dates, identifiers, categories, and amounts do not follow one standard.

03Duplicated

Repeated records inflate totals and create misleading patterns.

04Untraceable

Hidden transformation logic makes results difficult to reproduce or challenge.

The system

Build reliability into the analytical foundation.

The system does more than move data. It creates a visible chain from original records to the measures, segments, and trends used for review.

01 · Understand

Define the population.

Identify source systems, owners, relevant fields, time periods, and known limitations.

02 · Validate

Test before transforming.

Reconcile counts and totals, profile fields, and investigate missing or unexpected values.

03 · Transform

Make logic explicit.

Clean, standardize, join, classify, and calculate through repeatable SQL steps.

04 · Model

Preserve business meaning.

Structure relationships and measures so users can explore the data without changing the logic.

The operating flow

From raw records to a result an auditor can trace.

01ExtractBring source records into a controlled analytical environment.
02ProfileUnderstand completeness, formats, distributions, and anomalies.
03TransformApply documented cleaning, joins, classifications, and calculations.
04ReconcileCompare transformed output back to known counts and totals.
05PublishExpose approved measures and views for analysis.
Traceability is the control.

A reviewer should be able to follow a result back through the model, transformation logic, and original source population.

Design decisions

Reliability before visualization.

The strongest dashboard cannot compensate for an undefined population, incorrect joins, or calculations no one can reproduce.

01Why validate before transforming?

Profiling the original data creates a baseline for detecting missing records, unexpected formats, and transformation errors later in the process.

02Why use SQL for transformation?

SQL makes filtering, joining, aggregation, and classification logic explicit, repeatable, and easier to review than manual spreadsheet steps.

03Why separate the model from the dashboard?

A governed model creates consistent relationships and measures that can support multiple views without redefining the logic each time.

04Where does auditor judgment remain?

Auditors determine the relevant population, assess data reliability, define risk logic, investigate exceptions, and decide what the analysis means.

Portfolio demonstration

These projects use external or demonstration datasets and independent portfolio materials. They do not contain employer, client, or confidential information. Analytical results require validation before use in a formal audit environment.
