<section id="methods" class="sp-section sp-section--paper">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Analytical practice</p><h2>Use the population to improve where audit attention goes.</h2></div>
    <p>The analytical output is useful only when it changes scoping, walkthrough questions, test selection, evidence requests, or the interpretation of risk.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>Full-population testing</span><h3>Start with all relevant records when the data supports it.</h3><p>Define the risk and unit of analysis, reconcile the source population, apply explainable tests, stratify results, and direct detailed procedures toward higher-risk records and patterns.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>Analytical validation</span><h3>Make the analysis reproducible and challengeable.</h3><p>Document field definitions, assumptions, exclusions, thresholds, transformations, reconciliation steps, and record-level drill-through before relying on analytical results.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>Risk visualization</span><h3>Make patterns visible without overstating what they mean.</h3><p>Use trend, concentration, peer comparison, timing, and exception views to identify where activity differs—then investigate the business context before reaching a conclusion.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Execution practice</p><h2>Reduce recurring friction while preserving what requires judgment.</h2></div>
    <p>Standardization is most valuable when it removes mechanical inconsistency and gives auditors more time to understand risk, evaluate evidence, and communicate results.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-list-grid">
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Workflow improvement</span><h3>Observe → define → build → review → refine</h3><p>Identify repeated setup, recurring review notes, unclear ownership, and inconsistent documentation. Standardize the mechanics that should be repeatable while preserving flexibility where professional judgment is required.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Evidence sufficiency</span><h3>Keep conclusions traceable to support.</h3><p>Distinguish inquiry from validation, process narrative from control evidence, and analytical signals from confirmed exceptions. Retain enough support for a reviewer to understand what was tested and why the conclusion follows.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Manager review</span><h3>Review the reasoning—not only the formatting.</h3><p>Challenge whether the procedure addressed the risk, the evidence supports the assertion, exceptions were evaluated appropriately, and issue significance and root cause are well founded.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Professional judgment</span><h3>Keep accountability with the auditor.</h3><p>Templates, analytics, automation, and AI can organize information and accelerate analysis. They should not obscure assumptions, replace evidence evaluation, or own the final conclusion.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section sp-section--dark" id="responsible-ai">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker">Responsible AI</p><h2>Increase leverage with visible boundaries and human review.</h2></div>
    <p>AI is most useful in audit when its role is explicit, the source material remains visible, and a human reviewer retains responsibility for accuracy, evidence sufficiency, and the final decision.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-list-grid">
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Prepare</span><h3>Structure information.</h3><p>Use AI to organize transcripts, summarize provided source material, identify follow-up questions, and accelerate first-pass documentation.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Analyze</span><h3>Surface patterns for review.</h3><p>Use constrained prompts and analytical workflows to compare evidence, identify inconsistencies, and organize potential risk signals without treating generated output as evidence.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Review</span><h3>Validate against sources.</h3><p>Require the auditor to inspect source material, resolve unsupported claims, challenge assumptions, and determine whether the output is appropriate for the workpaper.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Own</span><h3>Keep accountability human.</h3><p>The auditor remains responsible for the procedure, evidence, professional judgment, documentation, and conclusion.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>
