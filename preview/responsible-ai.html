The point of view

Start with the audit problem—not the model.

AI is most useful when it improves a repeatable part of audit work. The objective is not more generated content. It is clearer evidence, stronger documentation, better questions, and more focused review.

The effectiveness test

Four conditions make a use case worth pursuing.

A compelling demonstration is not enough. The workflow must be bounded, repeatable, reviewable, and appropriately human-led.

01 · Source-bounded

We know what the AI may use.

Inputs and authoritative sources are identified before the task begins.

02 · Repeatable

The workflow solves a recurring need.

Defined inputs and outputs make the process reusable across similar work.

03 · Reviewable

A reviewer can inspect the work.

Evidence, interpretation, limitations, and open questions remain visible.

04 · Human-led

The auditor retains the decision.

AI supports mechanics and analysis; auditors determine sufficiency and conclusions.

One operating standard

Different use cases. The same visible chain of responsibility.

01SupplyAuditor-selected facts, evidence, scope, and limitations.
02ConstrainSource rules, non-fabrication instructions, and output structure.
03AssistAnnotation, organization, analysis, retrieval, or drafting support.
04ValidateAuditor checks support, logic, methodology, and conclusion.
The final gate is human.

AI may accelerate a task. It does not approve evidence, determine sufficiency, or make the audit decision.

Documentation example

Prompting becomes governance when boundaries are explicit.

Uncontrolled request

“Write an audit issue for missing approvals.”

The criteria, population, exceptions, cause, and impact are undefined.
  • May fill gaps with plausible assumptions
  • May overstate the available support
  • May hide what still requires auditor work
Audit-controlled request

Use supplied facts. Identify what is missing.

Do not invent criteria, exceptions, cause, impact, or management intent. Ask for clarification when required facts are absent.
  • Separates known facts from open questions
  • Creates a more reviewable draft
  • Preserves auditor validation and judgment

Selecting use cases

Useful before impressive.

The right question is not whether AI can perform a task. It is whether the workflow creates audit value without hiding risk or accountability.

01Does it solve a recurring audit problem?

Prioritize activities where structure, retrieval, comparison, or drafting consumes meaningful time across engagements.

02Can the output be verified?

Strong use cases produce work that an auditor can trace to evidence, methodology, or another authoritative source.

03Are the consequences of error manageable?

Use tighter controls—or avoid the workflow—when errors could expose confidential data, misstate conclusions, or bypass required approval.

04Is accountability still clear?

A reviewer should always know what the AI did, what evidence supported it, and who validated the final work.
