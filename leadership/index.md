<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">How I lead</p><h2>Four principles that shape the work.</h2></div>
    <p>The objective is a team that understands why the work matters, what evidence is required, and how to make sound audit decisions without unnecessary process.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>01 · Risk before procedure</span><h3>Start with the assurance need.</h3><p>Keep scope, controls, testing, evidence, and conclusions connected to the risk the engagement is intended to address.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>02 · Evidence before assertion</span><h3>Make the conclusion traceable.</h3><p>Require conclusions to remain supported by relevant, sufficient evidence and make the path from procedure to support to conclusion visible to the reviewer.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>03 · Review that develops judgment</span><h3>Improve the next decision—not only the current workpaper.</h3><p>Use manager review to explain the reasoning gap so auditors can apply the lesson independently on future work.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>04 · Improvement that fits the work</span><h3>Modernize where it improves quality or execution.</h3><p>Introduce methodology, analytics, automation, or AI when it materially improves coverage, consistency, reviewability, or efficiency.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>
