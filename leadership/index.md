<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">How I lead</p><h2>Six responsibilities that shape audit quality.</h2></div>
    <p>Strong management is not a final review step. It is a sequence of decisions that begins before the walkthrough and continues through remediation.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>01 · Risk alignment</span><h3>Start with the business risk—not the workpaper.</h3><p>Translate the audit objective into a focused scope, clear risk statements, and procedures that can produce useful assurance.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>02 · Engagement execution</span><h3>Create structure without over-engineering the work.</h3><p>Set expectations for walkthroughs, evidence, testing, documentation, issue development, and escalation.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>03 · Staff development</span><h3>Teach the reasoning behind the review note.</h3><p>Help auditors distinguish process narrative from controls, inquiry from validation, and evidence from conclusion.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>04 · Quality review</span><h3>Challenge what the evidence actually supports.</h3><p>Review work for risk alignment, sufficiency, traceability, consistency, and whether the stated conclusion is defensible.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--blue"><span>05 · Stakeholder communication</span><h3>Make the issue understandable and actionable.</h3><p>Connect the condition, risk, cause, and recommended action without losing nuance or overstating the result.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>06 · Continuous improvement</span><h3>Turn recurring friction into a better operating method.</h3><p>Use templates, analytics, automation, and AI where they improve consistency, coverage, or decision quality.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Management scope</p><h2>Experience across the full engagement lifecycle.</h2></div>
    <p>The work extends beyond testing. It includes the decisions that determine whether an audit is focused, supportable, and capable of influencing change.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-list-grid">
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Planning</span><h3>Risk assessment and scoping</h3><p>Define the audit objective, understand process ownership, identify the most significant risks, and align the planned procedures to the assurance need.</p><ul><li>Audit universe and plan development</li><li>Engagement-level risk assessment</li><li>Scope and control rationalization</li><li>Resource and timeline decisions</li></ul></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Execution</span><h3>Walkthroughs, testing, and evidence</h3><p>Structure walkthroughs to understand the process and evaluate controls without allowing inquiry to substitute for retained support.</p><ul><li>Process and control walkthroughs</li><li>Design and implementation assessment</li><li>Operating-effectiveness testing</li><li>Evidence sufficiency and traceability</li></ul></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Review</span><h3>Workpaper quality and conclusions</h3><p>Review whether the procedure was responsive to risk, the evidence is sufficient, and the conclusion accurately reflects what was tested.</p><ul><li>Manager review and coaching</li><li>Issue significance and root cause</li><li>Consistency across workpapers</li><li>Audit-defensible documentation</li></ul></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Influence</span><h3>Communication and remediation</h3><p>Translate audit work into clear communication, practical action, and follow-up that addresses the underlying risk.</p><ul><li>Business-partner communication</li><li>Executive and committee reporting</li><li>Management action plans</li><li>Remediation validation</li></ul></article>

  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Leadership principles</p><h2>What teams and stakeholders should expect.</h2></div>
    <p>These principles guide how I make decisions, review work, and introduce change.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-list-grid">
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Clarity</span><h3>Make expectations explicit.</h3><p>People produce stronger work when the objective, evidence standard, decision rights, and definition of completion are visible.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Judgment</span><h3>Do not automate responsibility.</h3><p>Tools can organize information and accelerate analysis. The auditor remains responsible for evaluating the evidence and owning the conclusion.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Practicality</span><h3>Adoption is part of the design.</h3><p>Methodology and technology should fit the environment, the team, and the risk—not add unnecessary process.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>Development</span><h3>Review should improve the next engagement.</h3><p>The strongest review notes explain the reasoning gap and help the auditor apply the lesson independently in future work.</p></article>
