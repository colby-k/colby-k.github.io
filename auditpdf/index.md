<section id="workflow" class="sp-section sp-section--paper">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">The audit problem</p><h2>Evidence annotation is simple work that becomes expensive when the workflow is wrong.</h2></div>
    <p>Auditors need to mark what was reviewed, show where support exists, explain notation consistently, and hand the work to a reviewer without creating another documentation layer.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-card-grid">
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--dark"><span>Audit notation</span><h3>Use a consistent evidence language.</h3><p>Audit-focused marks and references make the annotation useful to both the preparer and the reviewer instead of functioning as generic markup.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card"><span>Legend discipline</span><h3>Make every mark interpretable.</h3><p>Editable legend support keeps the meaning of evidence annotations visible rather than relying on undocumented conventions.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-card sp-card--signal"><span>Reviewability</span><h3>Keep the support easy to inspect.</h3><p>The workflow is designed to preserve a clear relationship between the source document, the auditor's notation, and the resulting review copy.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-section">
  <div class="sp-heading">
    <div><p class="sp-kicker sp-kicker--dark">Why I built it</p><h2>Purpose-built workflow can matter more than adding features.</h2></div>
    <p>AuditPDF reflects the same modernization principle I use elsewhere: standardize the repeatable mechanics, keep the evidence visible, and leave the judgment with the auditor.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="sp-list-grid">
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>01 · Evidence first</span><h3>Start from the support.</h3><p>Annotation should help explain what the auditor observed without becoming a substitute for the underlying evidence.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>02 · Lower friction</span><h3>Reduce context switching.</h3><p>Keep common audit annotation mechanics in one focused workspace instead of forcing a general PDF tool to behave like an audit workpaper tool.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>03 · Consistency</span><h3>Make conventions reusable.</h3><p>Repeatable marks and legend conventions make evidence review easier to understand across workpapers and reviewers.</p></article>
    <article class="sp-list-card"><span>04 · Portability</span><h3>Support practical document handling.</h3><p>A browser-based approach keeps the project lightweight and makes the workflow available beyond a single desktop application.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="sp-boundary"><strong>Portfolio boundary</strong><p>AuditPDF is an independent project built to explore a practical audit evidence workflow. It is not represented as an employer system or as a replacement for required workpaper, evidence-retention, or document-security controls.</p></section>
